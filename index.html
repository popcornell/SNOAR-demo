SNOAR: Self-training with Noise filtering, Observation adding, and Remixing for Speech Denoising Without Clean References

WHAM!CHiME-3 real

Unsupervised denoisers such as MixIT and noisy-target training learn directly from noisy recordings, avoiding the domain mismatch of synthetic training data, but remain well below supervised training. Teacher–student self-training, as in RemixIT, can close part of that gap without clean references.

  • Problem: The student is trained only toward the teacher's estimates, so teacher errors, in particular over-suppressed speech, cannot be recovered and are propagated: the regression analogue of confirmation bias in pseudo-labelling.
  • Proposed method: SNOAR (self-training with noise filtering, observation adding and remixing) corrects the teacher's estimates before they are used as self-supervision:
    • noise filtering: differential noise filtering (DNF) subtracts the teacher's own noise estimate, removing the noise it leaks into its speech estimate;
    • observation adding: combining the result with the noisy observation partly restores the suppressed speech and attenuates denoising artifacts.
  • Results (WHAM!): From a MixIT teacher, SNOAR reaches 13.25 dB SI-SDR, within 0.43 dB of a supervised topline, outperforms RemixIT on every metric, and has the lowest word error rate of all systems trained without clean references.
  • Results (CHiME-3 real): SNOAR improves every reference-free quality metric and the word error rate of three different teachers, whereas RemixIT falls below each of its teachers on every reference-free metric.
