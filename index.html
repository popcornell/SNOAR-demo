SNOAR: self-training for speech denoising without clean references

WHAM!CHiME-3 real

SNOAR corrects a teacher's estimates before using them as self-supervision: DNF removes the noise the teacher leaks, and observation anchoring (OA) restores part of the speech it suppresses. Below, every teacher is shown next to the two students trained from it: RemixIT and SNOAR. All systems are single-channel; apart from the supervised topline, none of them sees clean speech from the target domain.

WHAM! (simulated, tt set)

WSJ0 speech in real urban noise. Examples are chosen by rule, not by ear: first channel, 3–7 s, SNOAR(MixIT) SI-SDR improvement within the 40th–60th percentile of the test set (median 14.4 dB), one utterance per input-SNR bin. Every clip is level-normalised to −26 dBov, and all spectrograms of an example share one colour scale. RemixIT(MixIT) is shown at its dev-selected best checkpoint (step 52,316). Numbers are this clip’s own scores.

CHiME-3 (real recordings, et05)

Real tablet-microphone recordings in four environments; no clean reference exists, so only reference-free scores are shown. Three teachers differ in the clean speech they were allowed: none (MixIT on CHiME-3 alone), or out-of-domain WSJ0 / LibriSpeech synthetic mixtures. Students are shown at their dev-set selected checkpoints. Two recordings per environment, those whose SNOAR(MixIT) UTMOS gain over its teacher is closest to the median (+0.38).
